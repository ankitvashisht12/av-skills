# Rubric — Hacker News

A title. That's the deliverable. Manual submission only — there is no write
API, by design.

## The title

HN's own guideline : use the original title unless it is misleading or
linkbait. Editorialising is the most common reason a submission gets flagged
or retitled by moderators.

- No "How I…", no "Why you should…", no number-lists, no superlatives
- No question marks used as bait
- Strip marketing words entirely
- If the original title is already plain and descriptive, submit it unchanged
  and say so — a rewrite is not automatically an improvement

## Show HN

Only when the author built the thing and the reader can use it right now.
Then the submission points at **the tool** — its repo or its site — not at
the blog post about the tool. The blog post becomes the first comment,
written as context : what it does, why it exists, what it doesn't do.

## When to REJECT

Say no, plainly, when the post is personal-growth, habits, career reflection,
philosophy, or writing-about-writing. HN buries these, and a buried
submission costs the author more than not submitting.

`REJECT` is a successful output. Give one line of reasoning and stop. Do not
offer a "but you could try…" softener.

## Blockers

- [ ] Title ≤ 80 characters
- [ ] No editorialising, no clickbait, no superlatives
- [ ] `Show HN:` only if the author built it and it's usable now
- [ ] Show HN points at the tool, not the write-up
- [ ] Essay-type post → REJECT with a one-line reason
- [ ] Nothing from voice.md's banned list

## Nits

- First comment, if any, is context and caveats — never a sales pitch
- Note the best submission window (weekday mornings US Eastern)
- Warn that the author should not ask anyone to upvote; it's detectable and
  it's the fastest way to get penalised

## Score

- **9–10** — a title an HN reader would click without knowing the author
- **7–8** — accurate and plain, if unexciting
- **5–6** — subtly editorialised
- **≤4** — a blocker, or it should have been a REJECT
