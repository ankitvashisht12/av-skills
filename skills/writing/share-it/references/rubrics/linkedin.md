# Rubric — LinkedIn

Target: **400–700 characters**. Hard cap 3000, which you will never need.

A draft at 1400 characters is not a thorough draft, it is an unedited one.
Length here is a cost the reader pays, not effort the author shows.

## Format

Six to nine short lines, most of them one sentence. Not an essay — an essay
belongs on the blog this links to. Line breaks every one or two sentences; a
wall of text is scrolled past regardless of what it says.

**One idea.** Read voice.md's "Load" section and obey it literally. The
commonest failure of this rubric is a well-written post carrying four facts
where one would land.

**The truncation rule is the whole game.** LinkedIn hides everything after
roughly the first two lines behind "…see more". Those two lines are the
entire ad. They must work with the rest of the post invisible, and they must
create a reason to expand that the reader feels before they decide.

**No link in the body.** Put the URL in a separate first-comment block that
the card copies on its own button, and end the post with a plain pointer like
"link in the comments" — no arrow-spam, no "👇👇".

## Blockers

- [ ] First two lines stand alone and create tension
- [ ] Body ≤ 800 characters — over that, cut, don't justify
- [ ] The post carries **one** idea. A second supporting fact is fine; a
      method, a rigour claim and a regression proof stacked behind it is not
- [ ] Every sentence parses on one read, out loud, by a non-specialist
- [ ] Nothing described in prose that an attached screenshot already shows
- [ ] No URL anywhere in the post body
- [ ] No engagement bait of any kind (see voice.md)
- [ ] No emoji-bullet listicle
- [ ] Hook does not repeat the opening move of the last 3 LinkedIn hooks
- [ ] Nothing from voice.md's banned list
- [ ] The conclusion is withheld — the post is not a summary of the article
- [ ] First-comment block contains the URL and nothing but a line of context

## Nits

- A plain question, or a plain statement of what shipped, is a legitimate
  opening. The hook has to be true and openable — it does not have to be
  clever, and a clever one that needs decoding is worse than a flat one
- 3–5 hashtags on their own final line, broad + thematic + niche
- Unicode bold only if a single phrase genuinely needs it; never for headings
- One emoji maximum, and only where it carries meaning
- Paragraphs of 1–2 sentences
- A closing line that lands flat rather than asking a question

## Score

- **9–10** — the first two lines alone would make a stranger expand it, and
  nothing below them could be cut without losing the idea
- **7–8** — solid, sounds like the author, hook is merely competent
- **5–6** — reads like promo; the hook explains instead of provoking; or it's
  well written and simply too dense to finish
- **≤4** — a blocker is present, or it could have been written about any post

Density is scored as harshly as a bad hook. A 1400-character post that is
accurate, well-sourced and in-voice is a **5**, not an 8. Say so plainly:
"cut it in half and pick which half."
